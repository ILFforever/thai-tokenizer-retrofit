# Penalty decomposition (Week 2)

Generated by `src/run_decomposition.py`. Reference vocabulary for the counterfactuals: XGLM (`facebook/xglm-564M`). Evaluation slice: the first 1200 documents per domain, the same slice as `BASELINE.md`, plus the OPUS-100 parallel corpus.


**Columns.** *Pre-tok cuts* = share of consonant|mark junctions the pre-tokenizer cuts (a retrofit cannot repair these). *Cluster breaks* = share of Thai clusters (base + marks) split by the final tokens, byte-aware - replaces Week 1's mark-split rate. *Regex fix* = tok/100ch with the same vocabulary behind a mark-aware regex. *Best vocab* = tok/100ch if a strong Thai vocabulary were used inside this tokenizer's own pre-token pieces. *Pre-tok share* = fraction of the excess over the reference that no vocabulary can recover.

## What this shows

**1. Week 1's "mark-split rate" was not a pre-tokenization measure.** It
decoded one token id at a time, so byte-level fragments of Thai characters
(GPT-4, Qwen, BLOOM, Mistral's byte fallback) decoded to `U+FFFD` and could
never count as splits, and it could not tell a pre-tokenizer cut from a
missing merge. Reading each tokenizer's actual pre-tokenizer settles it:

- **cl100k (GPT-4) and Qwen2.5 cut 100% of consonant|mark junctions** in the
  pre-tokenizer. Their regex matches letters with `\p{L}+`; Thai vowel and
  tone marks are `\p{M}`, so every mark starts a new piece.
- **Every other tokenizer cuts 0%.** o200k added `\p{M}` to its regex; BLOOM
  splits on whitespace and punctuation only; Mistral and Typhoon do not
  pre-split at all; mT5, XGLM and WangchanBERTa split on spaces.

So Week 1's recommendation - retrofit cl100k because its mark-split rate was
"low" - was backwards: cl100k is one of the two tokenizers a retrofit
*cannot* fully fix.

**2. The two interventions behave very differently per tokenizer.**

| | Regex fix alone | Best possible vocabulary alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cl100k | 3.94x -> 3.94x (nothing) | 3.94x -> 2.25x |
| Qwen2.5 | 2.38x -> **1.42x** | 2.38x -> 2.25x |
| Mistral, BLOOM | nothing to fix | ~4x -> ~1.2x |

- cl100k needs **both**: its vocabulary was learned behind the broken regex,
  so it has no tokens that span a consonant and its mark, and fixing the
  regex alone changes nothing (92.4 -> 92.3 tok/100ch on Wikipedia).
- Qwen2.5 is the opposite: **89% of its excess is the regex.** Its
  vocabulary already contains 2,570 Thai entries, 1,578 of which (61%) its
  own pre-tokenizer can never emit - whole words such as `ประวัติศาสตร์`
  and `อย่างต่อเนื่อง`. A one-line regex change makes them reachable. Caveat:
  a token the shipped tokenizer never emits was never seen in training, so
  its embedding is probably untrained - tested in Week 6.
- Mistral and BLOOM have **100% vocabulary share**: nothing in the
  pre-tokenizer stands in the way, and a strong Thai vocabulary inside their
  own pre-token pieces reaches 1.13x (Mistral) and 1.24x (BLOOM).

**3. Week 1's finding #4 was overstated.** "Every tokenizer costs more on
social than on news" is false for Mistral (96.9 vs 99.9), BLOOM (93.9 vs
98.9) and GPT-4 (89.2 vs 93.7). It holds for the Thai-capable tokenizers
(o200k, Typhoon, XGLM, mT5, WangchanBERTa) and even for the reference
counterfactual (32.0 vs 28.6), which suggests the cost gap comes from
informal Thai itself (spelling variation, repeated characters, slang) once a
tokenizer has Thai vocabulary to lose - char-level tokenizers pay the same
per character everywhere.

## Target chosen: Mistral-7B

- **Damage is all in the vocabulary** (0% pre-tokenizer cuts, 100%
  vocabulary share), so a retrofit can reach all of it. Ceiling: 3.92x ->
  1.13x.
- **Highest token cost among fixable open-weight tokenizers** (3.92x;
  BLOOM is 4.25x but see below).
- **A like-for-like comparison exists.** Typhoon is Mistral-7B with its
  vocabulary *expanded* by 3,219 entries (all 32,000 Mistral ids unchanged).
  Retrofitting Mistral at a fixed 32,000 lets us compare fixed-size against
  expansion on the same base with the same token budget.
- Small vocabulary (32k) makes it the hard case for freeing slots without
  touching English - if it works here it works on larger vocabularies.

**Secondary target: BLOOM** (4.25x, 100% vocabulary share, 250k vocabulary).
Its 560M-parameter checkpoint runs on CPU, so it carries the embedding
experiment in Week 6, which Mistral-7B cannot do on this machine.

**Not chosen:** cl100k (blocked, and no open-weight model uses it); Qwen2.5
(the problem is the regex, not the vocabulary - reported as a separate
finding).
