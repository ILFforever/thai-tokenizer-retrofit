## What this shows

- **The curve flattens as the plan predicted.** Word candidates save ~37% of
  Mistral's Thai tokens at 1,000 units and ~58% at 5,000, but the marginal
  gain falls from 17% per 1,000 units (500-1,000) to 2.4% per 1,000
  (4,000-5,000). This matches Typhoon's and Csaki et al.'s report of
  diminishing returns past ~5,000 Thai tokens.
- **TCC units saturate at ~40%.** There are only ~5,000 distinct clusters
  and nearly all the saving comes from the first 1,000. Clusters are the
  right *atom* (a tokenizer should never split one) but too small to be the
  *target*: words and multi-cluster pieces are where the savings are.
- **Not a bound in either direction.** These projections assume each slot
  buys one whole word. In BPE a long word needs intermediate pieces too, but
  each piece (`ที่`, `การ`) also shortens every other word containing it, and
  pieces can span word boundaries. *Correction after Week 5:* the real
  retrofit beat this projection at every budget (e.g. 500 slots: -47% on
  Wikipedia against a projected -28%), so it understates what BPE achieves.
- **Register matters in the ranking.** `ไม่` (not) is 5x more frequent in
  social text than in Wikipedia, as are `แล้ว` (already) and the repetition
  mark `ๆ` (4x) - which Mistral cannot even encode as one token (it costs 3
  byte tokens). `จังหวัด` (province) is the reverse. A Wikipedia-only ranking
  would push the informal units down; equal domain weighting keeps them in.
- Projected reductions per domain are close (news slightly higher), so the
  equal weighting does not visibly favour one register at the candidate
  stage. Week 6 checks whether that survives the real retrofit.
