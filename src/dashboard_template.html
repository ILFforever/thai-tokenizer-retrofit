1 · The problem

Thai costs up to four times more tokens than English

On 3,000 sentence pairs with the same meaning in both languages, most tokenizers need far more tokens for the Thai side. The worst cases are open-weight models - the only ones whose tokenizer a deployment can change.

Thai penalty: Thai tokens per character ÷ English tokens per character

OPUS-100 English-Thai pairs (206k Thai, 230k English characters). 1.0× = Thai costs the same as English for the same content. WangchanBERTa is Thai-only and handles English badly, so its figure is below 1.

2 · Diagnosis

Two different faults, and only one of them is in the vocabulary

A tokenizer first cuts text into pieces with a pattern (the pre-tokenizer), then spells each piece with its vocabulary. No token can cross a pre-tokenizer cut. GPT-4's and Qwen2.5's pattern matches letters with \p{L}+, and Thai vowel and tone marks are \p{M} - so every mark is cut away from its consonant before the vocabulary is even consulted.

What each fix could reach, as a Thai penalty

"Regex fix" keeps the tokenizer's own vocabulary but lets marks stay with their consonant. "Best vocabulary" plugs a strong Thai vocabulary (XGLM's) into the tokenizer's own pre-token pieces - the most any vocabulary retrofit could achieve there.

6 · Against Typhoon

Same base model, same budget: fixed size matches expansion

Typhoon grew Mistral-7B's vocabulary from 32,000 to 35,219 entries. The retrofit keeps 32,000 and swaps instead. This compares tokenizers only - Typhoon also continued pretraining its model.

7 · Fairness

Informal Thai gains less - and loses more if you leave it out

Base Mistral spells Thai one character at a time, so it charges formal and informal writing the same. Every tokenizer that compresses Thai compresses formal text more. The retrofit's gap matches Typhoon's; a vocabulary trained on formal text alone nearly doubles it.

Informal ÷ formal cost (1.0 = same)

Formal = mean of Wikipedia and news; informal = social posts (WiseSight).

8 · Who pays

The freed slots belonged to other languages

"Unused" means unused by English, code and Thai. The slots come mostly from Cyrillic, Chinese, Japanese and Korean pieces, so those languages get more expensive. For a Thai-and-English deployment that price is zero; for a multilingual one it is not.

10 · Qwen2.5

1,578 Thai words in the vocabulary that the model has never seen

Qwen2.5 already has Thai words in its vocabulary, but its own pre-tokenizer can never produce them. Fixing the pattern would cut Thai tokens by up to 42% - but those rows were never trained, and repairing only the rows gets about three quarters of the way back. For Qwen, the one-line fix is not free.

Embedding row length (median)

All 1,578 unreachable rows sit on the same point as the 271 padding rows no token uses (within 0.1%). Trained rows are longer and spread out.

Bits per character on Thai (lower is better)

Qwen2.5-0.5B, 50 passages of ~600 characters per domain. Bits per character does not depend on how the text is split, so the tokenizers compare fairly.
